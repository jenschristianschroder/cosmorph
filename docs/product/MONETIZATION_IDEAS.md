# Cosmorph monetization ideas

Status: Exploratory product options

Audience: Product, design, engineering, security, operations, and development agents

Decision state: None of these ideas is approved for implementation by this document

## How an agent should use this document

This is an opportunity backlog, not a specification.

When a task concerns pricing, subscriptions, entitlements, premium features, paid content, creator tools, commerce, or billing:

1. Read this document for relevant options and constraints.
2. Identify the smallest opportunity that supports the stated product goal.
3. Explain user value, cost drivers, fairness implications, abuse risks, and dependencies.
4. Prefer reversible experiments over permanent platform complexity.
5. Do not implement a monetization option until an issue or product decision explicitly selects it.
6. Do not infer approval for pricing, a payment provider, a virtual currency, advertising, or a marketplace.

## Product principle

Cosmorph should monetize creation, expression, preservation, hosting, and enhanced observation. It should not sell survival, arbitrary Worldmind favor, or stronger simulation outcomes.

Free spectators must receive a complete and enjoyable experience. Paid features should deepen ownership, analysis, presentation, customization, or community operation.

## Non-negotiable guardrails

- Do not sell larger Warden action budgets, superior ecological outcomes, immunity from extinction, or priority treatment by the Worldmind.
- Do not let money purchase disasters or harmful interventions against another world or player.
- Do not sell undisclosed randomized rewards, loot boxes, or paid mystery outcomes.
- Do not use artificial scarcity, punitive streaks, confusing virtual currency, obstructed cancellation, preselected purchases, or other dark patterns.
- Do not make ordinary explanations of simulation outcomes a premium feature.
- Do not allow the Worldmind to initiate, recommend, negotiate, or execute purchases.
- Do not place billing credentials, payment data, or commerce tools inside the simulation or Worldmind capability boundary.
- If a payment lapses, preserve the Chronicle. Prefer a grace period followed by pause, read-only mode, or archival mode over deletion.
- Clearly distinguish recurring subscriptions, one-time purchases, usage allowances, and optional tips.
- Require explicit product, legal, privacy, security, and age-audience review before accepting money.

## Recommended commercial shape

The most natural starting structure is:

1. Free public spectatorship for discovery.
2. Paid hosting for people who create or operate persistent worlds.
3. An optional premium Observatory subscription.
4. Cosmetic customization.
5. Seasonal content packs after the core game is proven.
6. Chronicle exports and physical artifacts.
7. Community and educational plans after operational tooling matures.

Pricing mentioned below is a hypothesis for testing, not a commitment.

## Plan concepts

### Observer — free

Possible value:

- Watch public worlds.
- Follow a limited number of worlds, species, Wardens, or regions.
- View standard planetary overlays.
- Read recent Chronicle events.
- Participate in bounded predictions and moderated naming.
- Receive a limited recap.

Purpose:

- Make Cosmorph enjoyable before purchase.
- Build an audience for public worlds.
- Let spectators develop attachment before creating a world.

Do not intentionally degrade planet rendering or hide essential explanations to force an upgrade.

### Explorer — optional subscription

Possible pricing hypothesis: 5–8 USD per month, localized before launch.

Possible value:

- Longer Chronicle history.
- Advanced ecological overlays and comparisons.
- More followed entities and worlds.
- Cinematic camera controls and longer time-lapse playback.
- High-resolution screenshots.
- Additional documentary recaps.
- Personal Observatory themes and profile cosmetics.
- Cross-world dashboards and prediction history.

Risks:

- The free Observatory could become intentionally incomplete.
- AI-generated recaps could create unpredictable cost.
- Historical retention promises could outgrow storage policies.

### Worldkeeper — per-world subscription

Possible pricing hypothesis: 15–25 USD per month per active world, subject to measured cost.

Possible value:

- Create and operate one persistent world.
- Private, unlisted, or public visibility.
- A defined number of Warden slots.
- Invitations and spectator controls.
- Full Chronicle retention while active.
- Chapter recaps and world exports.
- A fixed monthly allowance of costly Worldmind or media-generation work.
- Cosmetic world-generation choices.

This is the strongest initial revenue candidate because customer value maps directly to persistent storage, scheduled simulation, AI usage, and support costs.

Questions to resolve:

- What happens after cancellation?
- Can a world be reactivated without losing elapsed time?
- Can ownership transfer?
- Can several people fund or operate one world?
- Is there a low-cost dormant archive tier?

### Community — subscription

Possible value:

- More Warden and moderator seats.
- A public community world.
- Community branding and landing page.
- Scheduled chapter premieres and events.
- Moderation dashboard and audit history.
- Stream overlays and creator-friendly views.
- Read-only webhooks or API access.
- Increased spectator capacity and media-export allowance.

Likely customers:

- Streamers.
- Online communities.
- Clubs.
- Small educational groups.
- Game-design and simulation communities.

Do not promise custom domains, service-level agreements, white labeling, or high concurrency until operational support exists.

### Education and exhibition — organization license

Possible value:

- Teacher or facilitator dashboard.
- Curated ecological scenarios.
- Pause, branch, compare, and reset controls.
- Classroom-safe spectator interaction.
- Learning objectives and guided questions.
- Exportable observations and reports.
- Museum or public-display presentation mode.
- Accessibility and content controls.

This should be validated with educators and exhibit operators before building specialized infrastructure.

## Monetization opportunity backlog

### 1. Persistent world hosting

Sell the ability to create, operate, preserve, and invite people into a world.

Implementation considerations:

- Active, paused, read-only, archived, and scheduled-for-deletion states.
- Grace periods and reactivation.
- Included AI and storage allowances.
- Ownership and operator roles.
- Export before deletion.
- Clear cost and retention policies.

### 2. Premium Observatory

Sell better ways to understand and present worlds:

- Advanced overlays.
- Historical comparison.
- Multi-world tracking.
- Cinematic tours.
- Custom dashboards.
- High-resolution capture.
- Longer time-lapse.
- Additional follows and alerts.

Keep the current world state, essential explanations, and basic accessibility free.

### 3. Season and content packs

Possible paid content:

- New biome families.
- Species and adaptation archetypes.
- Oceans, underground ecosystems, or intelligent life.
- New world-generation templates.
- Art, sound, atmosphere, and event-presentation themes.
- Curated starting scenarios.

Core simulation corrections, balance, compatibility, security, and accessibility updates remain free.

Seasonal content should persist after the sales window. Do not rely on fear of missing out.

### 4. Cosmetics

Possible items:

- Warden crests and visual identities.
- Planet palettes and atmosphere styles.
- Observatory themes.
- Chronicle frames and typography.
- Profile badges.
- Camera-transition styles.
- Cosmetic clouds, auroras, rings, moons, and orbital trails.

Cosmetics must not obscure state, undermine accessibility, or imply gameplay power.

Prefer direct purchase or included catalog access over randomized acquisition.

### 5. Chronicle media and keepsakes

Possible digital products:

- Illustrated world atlas.
- Species field guide.
- Chapter poster.
- High-resolution migration map.
- Narrated recap.
- Short documentary.
- Downloadable timeline.
- World data export in a documented format.

Possible physical products:

- Printed world history.
- Custom poster.
- Species cards.
- Evolutionary tree print.
- Globe or map artwork.

Start with digital exports. Physical fulfillment introduces taxes, shipping, refunds, damaged goods, vendor dependency, and customer support.

### 6. Premium AI media allowance

Charge for optional, costly presentation features rather than more favorable Worldmind decisions:

- Additional narrated documentaries.
- Alternate recap styles.
- Illustrated field-guide batches.
- Custom-length season summaries.
- Higher-resolution media generation.

Use fixed allowances for consumers. Consider metered overage only for organizations or creators who understand usage billing.

AI failure must not consume an allowance unless the requested artifact is delivered or automatically restored.

### 7. Community-funded worlds

Allow several people to support one public world:

- Shared subscription seats.
- Transparent funding goal.
- Gifted months of hosting.
- Community-selected cosmetic themes.
- Recognition wall or founder badge.

Funding must not translate into proportional control over ecological outcomes.

### 8. Gift worlds and founder packages

Possible offers:

- Gift one month of a private world.
- Founding-world package with a permanent cosmetic marker.
- Anniversary recap.
- Memorial or commemorative world with appropriate moderation.

Avoid lifetime hosting promises unless the financial and archival obligations are explicitly bounded.

### 9. Dormant archive or preservation endowment

Offer a low-cost state in which simulation pauses but the world remains viewable and can later be reactivated.

An optional prepaid preservation term could cover a fixed number of years. It must state the duration, export rights, and end-of-term behavior clearly. Do not market finite hosting as permanent.

### 10. Creator content packs

In a later phase, vetted creators could produce:

- Starting scenarios.
- Cosmetic planet themes.
- Documentary presentation packs.
- Curated species catalogs.
- Educational challenges.

A marketplace requires moderation, intellectual-property review, revenue sharing, refunds, content versioning, malware-safe asset formats, creator identity, tax handling, and removal procedures. Do not build it early.

### 11. Sponsored worlds or seasons

Possible partners:

- Conservation organizations.
- Science museums.
- Educational publishers.
- Nature-documentary brands.
- Universities and research outreach programs.

Sponsorship must be clearly disclosed and cannot covertly change Worldmind outcomes. Prefer themed public worlds, educational content, grants, or cosmetic presentation over programmatic advertising.

### 12. Research and read-only data access

Potential organizational product:

- Anonymized aggregate simulation datasets.
- Versioned world exports.
- Read-only APIs.
- Reproducible simulation runs.
- Classroom or research scenario comparison.

Never sell personal behavior, private-world content, prompts, agent instructions, or identifiable player data. Establish data rights and consent before positioning Cosmorph as a research platform.

### 13. Live premieres and special events

Possible value:

- Hosted season premiere.
- Guided chapter finale.
- Developer or naturalist commentary.
- Community prediction event.
- Curated multi-world documentary.

Keep ordinary viewing free. Paid tickets make sense only for labor-intensive hosted events, not routine world progression.

### 14. Optional patronage

Some spectators may want to support a favorite public world without buying features.

If offered:

- Make it clearly optional.
- Do not imply the world will be harmed without a tip.
- Show whether support funds hosting, creator activity, or a partner organization.
- Do not grant ecological control.

## Additional product ideas that support monetization indirectly

These are not purchases themselves, but improve willingness to pay:

- Beautiful offline recaps.
- Followable species, Wardens, lineages, and places.
- Shareable event cards and world links.
- Public world discovery and editorial curation.
- Chapter premieres and season archives.
- Reliable exports and account portability.
- Collaborative ownership roles.
- Strong moderation and privacy controls.
- Clear explanations of what the Worldmind decided and why the engine allowed it.
- Cost-aware quality settings for AI media.

## Architecture implications

Do not tightly couple game code to a payment provider.

Suggested conceptual boundaries:

- Catalog: stable product and price identifiers.
- Entitlement: what an account, world, or organization may use.
- Subscription state: provider status mapped into an internal state machine.
- Allowance: renewable limits for costly features.
- Usage ledger: immutable, idempotent records of metered work.
- Purchase ledger: immutable references to provider transactions, refunds, disputes, and grants.
- Commerce adapter: checkout and webhook integration outside the domain simulation.
- Access policy: converts entitlements into capabilities.

Requirements:

- The Worldmind and deterministic simulation never call the commerce adapter.
- A payment webhook cannot directly mutate a world. It changes entitlement state through an idempotent application command.
- Verify webhook signatures and replay protection.
- Treat duplicate and out-of-order events as normal.
- Keep provider customer identifiers separate from public player identifiers.
- Never store card data.
- Use a provider-hosted checkout initially.
- Support manual grants, refunds, disputes, grace periods, and customer-support audit.
- Evaluate entitlements server-side. The browser may display them but cannot assert them.
- Preserve base spectator access during a temporary billing-provider outage.
- Meter expensive work before execution and reconcile after delivery.
- Feature-flag every commercial capability.
- Build a no-payment test provider for local and automated testing.

## Cost model to instrument before pricing

Measure per world and per account:

- Worldmind requests, input tokens, cached input, and output tokens.
- AI media-generation attempts and successful artifacts.
- Simulation job vCPU-seconds and memory-seconds.
- Blob capacity, operations, and retention growth.
- API requests, network egress, and media delivery.
- Logging and monitoring ingestion.
- Payment fees, refunds, disputes, taxes, and support cost.

Useful derived metrics:

- Monthly cost per active world.
- Monthly cost per spectator.
- AI cost per chapter and season.
- Chronicle storage growth per world-year.
- Gross margin by plan.
- Cost of free spectatorship.
- Percentage of paid allowance delivered successfully.
- Cost and recovery rate of dormant worlds.

Do not commit to pricing until representative worlds have run long enough to measure quiet periods, disasters, chapter transitions, spectator spikes, and media generation.

## Experiments before full implementation

1. Add cost telemetry without a checkout flow.
2. Ask early users which artifact or capability they would miss most.
3. Test a fake pricing page measuring plan interest without collecting payment.
4. Offer a manually administered founding-world pilot.
5. Test one paid digital Chronicle export before physical products.
6. Compare interest in premium observation against interest in world ownership.
7. Pilot one community world with manual moderation.
8. Interview educators before building classroom controls.

Success criteria should include user satisfaction, retention, fairness perception, support burden, and gross margin—not conversion alone.

## Questions requiring explicit decisions

- Who is the intended age audience?
- Is Cosmorph browser-only, or will it later ship through Steam or app stores?
- Who legally owns a world and its generated Chronicle?
- What export rights exist?
- What happens to a world on cancellation, payment failure, account suspension, or owner death?
- Can ownership or billing responsibility transfer?
- Are public worlds funded by an owner, a community, Cosmorph, or a sponsor?
- Which AI work is included, queued, degraded, or unavailable after an allowance is exhausted?
- Which seasonal content affects existing worlds?
- Are prices localized?
- Which regions are supported for tax, refunds, and consumer-protection compliance?
- Can users create content that is sold to others?
- Will organizations receive contractual retention or service commitments?

## Suggested implementation order

1. Cost and usage instrumentation.
2. Internal entitlement model with a fake provider.
3. World active, paused, archived, and grace-period lifecycle.
4. Founding Worldkeeper pilot using provider-hosted checkout.
5. Premium Chronicle export.
6. Explorer subscription after spectator retention is demonstrated.
7. Cosmetics and season packs.
8. Community plan.
9. Education, sponsorship, marketplace, and data products only after validation.
